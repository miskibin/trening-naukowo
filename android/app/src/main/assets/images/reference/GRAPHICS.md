# Anatomy reference images

These four images are offline study references for the original Polish teaching plates. They are detailed reference material, not decorative hero art. The embedded labels are English, so explain them in Polish beside the image. Source images are bundled unchanged.

## `upper-limb-muscles-openstax.webp`

- **Figure:** “Muscles That Move the Forearm,” Figure 11.25, *Anatomy and Physiology*, OpenStax.
- **Useful for:** Seeing the biceps short and long heads, brachialis, and brachioradialis in anatomical context.
- **Limit:** It labels the muscle heads but does not name the exact bony origin points or insertions. Pair it with the focused bone views and the lesson labels.
- **Source page:** https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology/pages/11-5-muscles-of-the-pectoral-girdle-and-upper-limbs
- **Direct image:** https://openstax.org/apps/image-cdn/v1/f%3Dwebp/apps/archive/20260604.144757/resources/6668b272a691b8377071de429a1336fec0469a5c
- **Credit and license:** OpenStax, *Anatomy and Physiology*, Figure 11.25, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Credit OpenStax and preserve the license terms when redistributing.

## `proximal-radius-landmarks-commons.png`

- **Figure:** Anterior view of the radius with a full-bone overview and enlarged proximal detail.
- **Useful for:** Distinguishing the radial head from the radial tuberosity. The tuberosity is shown below the neck, rather than at the radial head.
- **Limit:** Labels are English; the illustration does not show the biceps tendon attaching to the tuberosity.
- **Source page:** https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Radius_Anteiror_Proximal.png
- **Direct image:** https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/7d/Radius_Anteiror_Proximal.png
- **Credit and license:** DrJanaOfficial, CC BY-SA 4.0. Keep attribution and share-alike terms if adapting it.

## `forearm-attachments-gray213.png`

- **Figure:** Plate 213 from Henry Gray, *Anatomy of the Human Body* (1918), showing the radius and ulna with muscle attachments.
- **Useful for:** Comparing the biceps attachment on the radius with brachialis and forearm muscle attachment regions on the ulna, and seeing brachioradialis at the distal radius.
- **Limit:** A dense historical line drawing; the small English lettering is harder to read on a phone than the original Polish teaching diagram.
- **Source page:** https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gray213.png
- **Direct image:** https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ac/Gray213.png
- **Credit and license:** Henry Gray (1918), public domain.

## `pectoralis-major-openstax-11-23.webp`

- **Figure:** “Muscles That Move the Humerus,” Figure 11.23, *Anatomy and Physiology*, OpenStax.
- **Useful for:** Full anterior-lateral context for the pectoralis major's broad chest origin and path toward the humerus; a second panel labels the coracoid process on the scapula.
- **Limit:** It is not an origin/insertion close-up and does not mark the pectoralis major humeral footprint precisely. Use it for orientation and verify the exact footprint separately in the Polish plate.
- **Source page:** https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology/pages/11-5-muscles-of-the-pectoral-girdle-and-upper-limbs
