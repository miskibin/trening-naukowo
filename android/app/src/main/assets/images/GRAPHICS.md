# Offline anatomy reference figures

These two detailed figures are bundled as optional reference material for lessons. They are useful at reading/zoom detail and should sit below the lesson’s original Polish teaching diagrams, rather than serve as hero or decorative artwork. Both source figures use English labels; explain them in Polish in the surrounding lesson instead of altering the originals.

## `motor-units.png`

- **Figure:** “Motor Unit and Pool,” Figure 40.8, by Casey Henley.
- **What it teaches:** One alpha motor neuron branches to innervate multiple muscle fibers; the neuron and its fibers form one motor unit. Several motor units innervating the muscle together form a motor pool. Colors distinguish the three example units.
- **Limitations:** Schematic and not to scale. It does not show recruitment order, force grading, or the neuromuscular-junction detail; those need separate teaching material.
- **Source page:** [Introduction to Neuroscience — Spinal Motor Control and Proprioception](https://openbooks.lib.msu.edu/introneuroscience1/chapter/spinal-control-of-movement/)
- **Direct image:** https://openbooks.lib.msu.edu/app/uploads/sites/87/2022/07/MotorUnitAndPool-1024x442.png
- **Credit and license:** “Motor Unit and Pool” by Casey Henley, licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. [License text](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/).

## `sliding-filaments.webp`

- **Figure:** “The Sliding Filament Model of Muscle Contraction,” Figure 10.10, *Anatomy and Physiology 2e*, OpenStax.
- **What it teaches:** Side-by-side relaxed/contracted sarcomere states; actin and myosin overlap more as Z lines approach, the I bands and H zone narrow, and the A band (thick-filament length) stays constant.
- **Limitations:** A static, schematic comparison; it does not animate cross-bridge cycling or explain calcium/ATP steps by itself. The English labels need Polish explanation in the lesson.
- **Source page:** [OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e — 10.3 Muscle Fiber Contraction and Relaxation](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-3-muscle-fiber-contraction-and-relaxation)
- **Direct image:** https://openstax.org/apps/image-cdn/v1/f%3Dwebp/apps/archive/20260604.144757/resources/1463f0ef934552382ad130bf14cd23dd9e1e779a
- **Credit and license:** OpenStax, *Anatomy and Physiology 2e*, Figure 10.10, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. [OpenStax licensing information](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/1-introduction).

## Supplementary detailed anatomy references

These are reference illustrations for close inspection inside the learning app, not hero art.

### Forearm pronation and supination

- Bundled file: pronation-supination-openstax.webp
- Image: [OpenStax image asset](https://openstax.org/apps/image-cdn/v1/f%3Dwebp/apps/archive/20260604.144757/resources/97c104133b63cc422f9c84541c4a49651dc506b5)
- Source: [Fundamentals of Nursing, §22.1, Table 22.3](https://openstax.org/books/fundamentals-of-nursing/pages/22-1-importance-of-activity-movement-and-alignment)
- Credit: OpenStax, adapted from Anatomy and Physiology 2e. Licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; retain this attribution and license notice for redistribution.
- Teaching use: The two views explicitly label the radius crossing over the ulna in pronation and the parallel bones in supination.
- Limitation: Labels are in English and the simplified diagram does not depict soft tissue or the proximal/distal radioulnar joints.

### Distal biceps insertion

- Bundled file: biceps-radial-tuberosity-cureus.png
- Image: [Cureus Figure 1 image asset](https://assets.cureus.com/uploads/figure/file/1798152/article_river_13b6d240c93a11f0854adb25be14521f-Figure-1.png)
- Source: [Elgendy et al., “Biceps Tendon Rupture: Contemporary Evidence, Evolving Techniques, and a Mechanics-to-Management Clinical Framework,” Cureus 17(11), e97718 (2025), Fig. 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12740767/)
- Credit: Figure is an original hand-drawn illustration by the article authors. Article is open access under CC BY 4.0; cite the authors and article when redistributing the figure.
- Teaching use: Shows the biceps tendon ending at the radial tuberosity and the bicipital aponeurosis extending into forearm fascia.
- Limitation: This is a clinical review illustration rather than a general anatomy textbook plate; the pencil rendering is schematic and should be paired with a clear explanation that the distal biceps inserts on the radius.
